Built by following every vehicle minute to minute and asking where its deviation changed. Cells with under 40 transits are dropped. Read it as where the timetable is won and lost, not purely as congestion: a terminus with recovery time built in will show blue because the schedule allows it, not because the road is fast.
Which routes bunch
Share of observations where the gap to the vehicle ahead had collapsed to under half that route's usual gap for the hour. Across the whole dataset, not this frame.
Frequent service only: route-hours whose usual gap is 15 minutes or less. On a route running every 40 minutes with four vehicles, half the median gap is the timetable's own irregularity rather than bunching, and it scores higher than anything real.

Source: the City of Vilnius live vehicle feed (stops.lt/vilnius/gps_full.txt) joined to the static GTFS timetable, published by SĮ "Susisiekimo paslaugos" (JUDU). Deviation is the operator's own NuokrypisSekundemis field, where positive means behind schedule. A vehicle reports no deviation when the operator has no timetabled trip matched to it; those are drawn as hollow rings. Measured over this dataset, 74% of them are heading to or from a depot and 26% are on an ordinary passenger destination, so "no deviation" means unmatched rather than specifically depot-bound. Before about 04:40 fewer than 120 vehicles are on a timetable, so the 90th percentile swings on single vehicles there and should be read loosely. The grey web underneath is not a street map: it is the route network itself, drawn from the city's own shapes.txt, so every line is a road a bus actually runs on. The blue line is the Neris, traced by ferry route L1.
