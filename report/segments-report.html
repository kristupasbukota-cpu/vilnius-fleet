7. What this does and does not do

Every live row carries MatavimoLaikas, when the GPS actually fixed, and NuokrypisSekundemis, how many seconds late the vehicle was at that moment. Their difference is the vehicle's position on its own timetable: a bus six minutes late at 17:20 is standing where it was scheduled to be at 17:14. Looking that up in the trip's stop times says which pair of stops it is between, without touching a single coordinate. Between two fixes, the gap between how much real time passed and how much schedule time passed is the lateness gained, and it is charged to whichever scheduled segments the vehicle was crossing while it gained it.

The arithmetic was checked against the geometry it never uses. On 76,407 sampled readings the vehicle's nearest stop was the claimed segment's own start or end 98.9% of the time, split 46.5% / 52.4% between the two ends, which is what "somewhere between them" should look like. The median vehicle sat 6 m off the straight line joining them.

Caveats worth knowing

Scheduled times are published to the minute, so a segment scheduled for 60 seconds carries about half a minute of quantisation; this averages out over hundreds of traversals but makes any single short segment unreliable. A segment is only counted once the vehicle is seen to leave it, so half-observed traversals at the edges of an observation chain are discarded rather than counted short. Up to four published versions of the timetable that could be in force on a day are read, so trips the city drops when it republishes are still recognised.

One expectation this did not confirm, measured on 17 and 18 August. Scheduled recovery time was assumed to sit at the ends of routes, which would make a terminus look like a place where traffic is kind. It does not: segments that give time back sit at median position 0.48 through their trip at a median scheduled speed of 18.8 km/h, against 0.47 and 18.1 km/h for segments that lose it. The padding in this timetable is spread evenly along the route, not parked at the end.
