1. The map

Straight lines between consecutive stops, not the roads themselves. Hover any segment for its numbers.

The damage is not spread evenly across the city. It concentrates on the Neris crossings and the Šnipiškės approach: Kražių street into the Opera, Lvivo street out towards Rinktinės and Tuskulėnai, Čiurlionio into Tumo-Vaižganto, and Žaliasis tiltas itself. Four of the six worst pieces of road in the city are within about 900 m of that bridge.

The Sunday column is the whole argument. These are not slow roads. They are roads that fail under commuter load. Kražių street into the Opera costs a bus 2 min 11 s on a Monday and 5 seconds on a Sunday, on the same 409 m, to the same timetable.

4. Is it real?

A segment's mean is only worth reading if the same segment does the same thing on a different day. Monday and Tuesday are two independent working days, collected by two independent machines. Each cell counts segments; the diagonal is perfect agreement.

5. How concentrated is it?

There is a corridor and there is a long tail. Fixing the ten worst segments would recover about a seventh of the loss; you would need roughly 400 of them for three quarters.

6. What this does and does not do

Every live row carries MatavimoLaikas, when the GPS actually fixed, and NuokrypisSekundemis, how many seconds late the vehicle was at that moment. Their difference is the vehicle's position on its own timetable: a bus six minutes late at 17:20 is standing where it was scheduled to be at 17:14. Looking that up in the trip's stop times says which pair of stops it is between, without touching a single coordinate. Between two fixes, the gap between how much real time passed and how much schedule time passed is the lateness gained, and it is charged to whichever scheduled segments the vehicle was crossing while it gained it.

The arithmetic was checked against the geometry it never uses. On 76,407 sampled readings the vehicle's nearest stop was the claimed segment's own start or end 98.9% of the time, split 46.5% / 52.4% between the two ends, which is what "somewhere between them" should look like. The median vehicle sat 6 m off the straight line joining them.

Caveats worth knowing

Monday is missing 10:00 to 13:59: the laptop collecting it lost wifi and the cloud machine did not exist yet. Tuesday stops at 15:50, so it has no evening peak, which makes the 0.854 agreement a statement about the whole day rather than about the peak. Scheduled times are published to the minute, so a segment scheduled for 60 seconds carries about half a minute of quantisation; this averages out over hundreds of traversals but makes any single short segment unreliable. A segment is only counted once the vehicle is seen to leave it, so half-observed traversals at the edges of an observation chain are discarded rather than counted short. Two published versions of the timetable straddle the observation window and both are read, 22,408 trips between them.

One expectation this did not confirm. Scheduled recovery time was assumed to sit at the ends of routes, which would make a terminus look like a place where traffic is kind. It does not: segments that give time back sit at median position 0.48 through their trip at a median scheduled speed of 18.8 km/h, against 0.47 and 18.1 km/h for segments that lose it. The padding in this timetable is spread evenly along the route, not parked at the end.
