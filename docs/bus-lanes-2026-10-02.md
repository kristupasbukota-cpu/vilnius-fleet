# Do the bus lanes sit where the delay is?

2 October 2026. Task #6.

**Result: the bus lanes are on the busiest bus roads, but most of the worst places
have none.** Roads with a bus lane carry 18% of all stop-to-stop hops on 5% of the
measured network's length, so the lanes are well aimed at where buses are. But of
the 20 links that lose the most time, only 5 have a lane alongside, morning or
evening, and the city's single worst link in the evening peak, Čiurlionio st. to
Tumo-Vaižganto st., has none in either direction.

18 autumn working days, Monday to Thursday, 2 September to 1 October. 1,766
stop-to-stop links with at least 200 hops.

---

## 1. The lane data

The city's own layer, **"Viešojo transporto A juostos (Esamos)"**, published by
Vilniaus planas on its ArcGIS server (`gis.vplanas.lt`, Transportas_public,
layer 9): 78 sections, **42.5 km** of lane geometry, 19.5 km plain A lanes and
23.0 km A+ lanes that electric cars and motorcycles may also use. JUDU's own figure
is 46.1 km; the difference is presumably lanes opened since the layer was last
updated, or counting conventions. Five sections apply only on working days 07:00 to
20:00; the rest at all times.

OpenStreetMap, checked first, holds only about 18 km of bus lanes for Vilnius and was
not used.

**One limitation matters.** The layer does not say which direction of traffic each
lane serves, and its lines cannot settle it: 32 of the 56 lane sections near a bus
link sit within 30 m of stop links in both directions. So a link here "has a lane" if
a lane runs alongside it, in either direction. That overstates coverage. A link with
no lane by this test has none in either direction.

A link is matched when most of nine points along it lie within 30 m of a lane
running within 30° of parallel. 122 of the 1,766 links match.

## 2. Coverage against delay

| | share of hops | share of link length | share of time lost | delay per hop, lane links | delay per hop, other links |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all day | 18.2% | 5.3% | 16.9% | +2.45 s | +4.07 s |
| morning 07-09 | 18.6% | 5.3% | 16.4% | +7.43 s | +10.38 s |
| evening 16-19 | 18.5% | 5.3% | 17.6% | +6.38 s | +8.79 s |

**The lanes are where the buses are.** A twentieth of the length carries nearly a
fifth of the traffic.

**Links with a lane lose less per hop than links without**, about 3 s less in both
peaks. That is consistent with the lanes working, but it cannot prove it: the lanes
were built where they were wanted, and this compares different roads, not the same
road before and after.

Morning peak, the top two are the same pair: Rūdninkų st. to MO muziejus (4.5, lane)
and Čiurlionio st. to Tumo-Vaižganto st. (3.3, no lane).

**The approaches to Žaliasis tiltas and the river crossings are only partly
covered.** Europos aikštė to the bridge has no lane alongside; Kalvarijų turgus to
the bridge does. The links along Rinktinės, Šeimyniškių and Vasaros streets, north of the
river, have none.

**Having a lane is not enough on the worst link of all.** Rūdninkų st. to MO muziejus
has one and is still first or second in both peaks.

## What it means

The lane network is aimed at volume, and it reaches a fifth of all bus traffic. It is
not aimed at the specific places where buses lose the most time: three quarters of
the worst links have no lane alongside. If new lanes are to be placed by the data,
this list is where to start, beginning with Čiurlionio st. to Tumo-Vaižganto st.,
the approaches to Žaliasis tiltas from Europos aikštė, and the Šeimyniškių st.
corridor.

Combined with the 2 October finding that the autumn morning problem is inbound, an
inbound peak-hours lane on those links would aim at both.

## Method

Lane geometry: `https://gis.vplanas.lt/arcgis/rest/services/Interaktyvus_zemelapis2/Transportas_public/MapServer/9`,
queried 2 October 2026, all 78 features. Links: straight lines between consecutive
stops from the archived timetables, delays from `analysis/trav-*.csv.gz`. A link has
a lane if at least five of nine points along it are within 30 m of a lane segment
running within 30° of parallel. Time lost is the sum of positive and negative
delay per link; shares of time lost use links with net positive loss.
