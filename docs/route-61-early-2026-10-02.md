# Route 61's early departures are two vehicles

2 October 2026. Task #23, the last open question in the early-departure thread.

**Result: route 61 direction 1 leaves early because two of the six vehicles that
run it leave early, not because of the timetable or the data.** On the same
scheduled trips, vehicles 7020 and 7003 left more than a minute early on 29 of 72
departures. Vehicle 7017, running the same two blocks on other days, did so on 0 of
36. The timetable makes it easy: it gives direction 1 a turnaround of one minute,
often none, at Kuprioniškės.

Four days, 22 and 24 to 26 August, the only days with per-trip departure tables
(`analysis/dep-*.csv.gz`). Early means the deviation at the scheduled departure,
taken at both bays where a loop has two, reads more than 60 s ahead, the same
measure as the network's 1.85%.

---

## 1. The route against itself

| | departures | early > 60 s |
|---|---|---|
| route 61 direction 0 | 164 | 3.0% |
| route 61 direction 1 | 167 | **19.2%** |
| network | 37,870 | 1.8% |

The artefacts already found elsewhere do not apply here. Direction 1 starts at 7874
Kuprioniškės, which has no second bay, and its first hop is 398 m to Vilties st., so
the timing-point defect at Vilkpėdės žiedas has no equivalent.

## 2. Who leaves early

Direction 1, by vehicle:

| vehicle | departures | early > 60 s | median deviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7020** | 36 | **19 (53%)** | -65.5 s |
| **7003** | 55 | **13 (24%)** | -30.0 s |
| 7017 | 36 | 0 | 0 |
| 7059 | 20 | 0 | 0 |
| 7046 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| 7054 | 10 | 0 | 0 |

**All 32 early departures belong to two vehicles.** The other four, 76 departures
between them, have none.

The comparison that settles it uses the two blocks those vehicles share. Blocks
`A61-01-1` and `A61-02-1` are the same scheduled trips every day, driven by 7003,
7017 or 7020 depending on the day:

| vehicle, same two blocks | departures | early > 60 s |
|---|---|---|
| 7020 | 36 | 19 |
| 7003 | 36 | 10 |
| 7017 | 36 | **0** |

Same trips, same timetable, same terminus. One vehicle never leaves early, and the
other two do so on 29 of 72 departures.

The early cases are not a fixed offset. They spread from -62 s to -283 s, with a
median of -77 s and the middle half between -96 and -71 s, and they are spread
across the day, from 05:00 to after midnight. A clock error would show a constant offset everywhere.
These vehicles show nothing unusual on route 61 direction 0, early on 5% and 2% of
departures, or on route 13, which they also run, early on 0 to 8%.

## 3. Why direction 1, and why there

The timetable, version of 24 August:

| | scheduled turnaround before departure | scheduled running time |
|---|---|---|
| direction 0 | median 13 min | 27 min |
| direction 1, from Kuprioniškės | **median 1 min, often 0** | 16 min |

Direction 1 leaves Kuprioniškės a minute or less after the previous trip arrives.
And it does not keep time once under way: over 44 days of the archive, direction 1
trips end a median of **3.5 to 7.5 minutes late** for every vehicle on the route.
Direction 0, with its long turnaround to absorb that, ends roughly on time.

So a driver arriving at Kuprioniškės slightly early, as 7003 and 7020 do (median
-28 s and -13 s at the end of direction 0), is looking at a one-minute stand before
a trip that will run late regardless. Two of them pull straight out. The
first-sighting deviation agrees: their early trips already read a median of -74 s
three minutes before the scheduled departure, while the departures that left on time read
-2 s.

## 4. What this is and is not

**It is genuine early departure.** A passenger at Kuprioniškės or Vilties st. who
arrives on time for a 61 run by 7020 had about an even chance of seeing it already
gone.

**It is not a property of the route, the stop or the timetable alone.** The same
timetable is kept by four of six vehicles. The timetable makes it easy; the
behaviour is specific.

**It cannot say whether it is the vehicle or the driver.** The feed identifies
vehicles, not people. A driver regularly assigned to these two vehicles would look
exactly like this.

**It is four August days.** Per-trip departure tables exist only for 22 to 26
August. Whether this continues on the autumn network, and whether the same two
vehicles still account for it, needs `departure.py` run on September days. That is
the next step, and it needs the box.

## Corrections this makes

The 24 September note called route 61 direction 1 "the clearest open question in
this thread" and the 20 September note said it "does not fit" the timing-point
explanation. Both stand. This answers the question: it was never the stop.

## Method

```python
d = pd.concat(pd.read_csv(p) for p in glob("analysis/dep-2026-08-2*.csv.gz"))
# both bays: the later reading wins where a loop has two
d["both"] = np.where(d.dev_at_timing.notna() & (d.dev_at_timing != d.dev_at_departure),
                     np.maximum(d.dev_at_departure, d.dev_at_timing), d.dev_at_departure)
d["early"] = d.both < -60
r = d[(d.route.astype(str) == "61") & (d.direction == 1)]
r.groupby("vehicle").early.agg(["sum", "size"])
```

Turnarounds: `gtfs/gtfs-20260824.zip`, the gap between each trip's last scheduled
arrival and the next trip's first departure in the same `block_id`. End-of-trip
deviation: `dev1` in `analysis/blocks-*.json.gz`, 3,270 route 61 trips over 44 days.
