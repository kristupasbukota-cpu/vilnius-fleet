# Rain, and what it was hiding

> **Audited 1 October 2026.** The record and its timing check out against Vilnius
> airport's own weather reports, and the hour-level rain effect, the autumn jump and
> the dry-morning easing all hold with the airport as the instrument. Two statements
> below were wrong and are corrected in place, and the day-level wet-morning figure
> of +2.96 depends on which instrument defines a wet morning (+1.44, interval
> including zero, with the airport). See [`weather-audit-2026-10-01.md`](weather-audit-2026-10-01.md).

30 September 2026. Task #12: join weather to the record.

**Result: a wet hour costs the network about a third more delay than the same hour
dry, and weather explains none of the autumn morning jump.** With rain accounted
for, the morning has not just settled: on dry days it has **eased by about a quarter
since the first school week**, which the wet weeks at the end of September had been
masking.

From tonight the record carries its own weather. `weather.py` fetches hourly
observations every night alongside the bus data, and the whole archive back to
14 August is already filled in under `weather/`.

---

## 0. The weather data

Hourly observations from **Vilniaus AMS**, the Lithuanian Hydrometeorological
Service's automatic station (54.626 N 25.107 E, 13.4 km west-south-west of
Cathedral Square; *corrected 1 October, this said "about 9 km south-west of the old
town"*), through the service's open API. **Source: Lietuvos hidrometeorologijos
tarnyba (LHMT), api.meteo.lt, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0.** 1,147 hours, 14 August to
30 September, no gaps, 162.0 mm of rain in total. *(Those 1,147 ran to 18:00 UTC on
30 September; the complete record to midnight is 1,152 hours and the same 162.0 mm.)*

One station for a whole city is a limitation. A shower can soak Žirmūnai and miss
a station 13 km to the west. *(Corrected 1 October: this said "miss the airport"; the
station is not at the airport.)* That noise works against finding an effect, not for it, so anything that
shows through it is, if anything, understated.

An hour counts as **wet** at 0.1 mm or more. Each observation covers the hour
ending at its timestamp, and is matched to the bus hour it covers in local time.

## 1. A wet hour, inside its own day

The cleanest test compares each hour with the other hours of the same day. Every day
gets its own level, and every hour of the day gets its own level separately for
summer and autumn, so that the busy-hour pattern and any day-to-day mood of the city
are both taken out. What remains is: on a given day, at a given hour, does it matter
whether it was raining?

Working days 18 August to 29 September, 1 September excluded, 06:00 to 22:00. 480
day-hours, 30 days, 73 of the hours wet.

| | seconds per hop | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **a wet hour** | **+1.31** | **[+0.26, +2.17]** |
| wet in the morning peak, 07-08 | +2.22 | [-1.56, +5.60] |
| wet in the evening peak, 16-18 | +2.73 | [+0.47, +4.99] |
| wet off-peak | +0.74 | [+0.07, +1.57] |

Intervals are a bootstrap over whole days, 1,000 resamples.

Against the typical delay of each window, 6.5 s in the morning peak, 7.7 s in the
evening and 2.7 s off-peak, **rain adds roughly 25 to 35% everywhere.** The morning
interval is wide only because there were eleven wet morning-peak hours to learn from;
its point estimate is in line with the others.

**The road stays slow after the rain stops.** With the previous hour included, rain
this hour adds +1.06 [+0.09, +1.93] and rain the hour before adds another +0.96
[+0.17, +1.77].

**Checks.**

- **Placebo.** Giving every day another day's weather, 500 times, returns a mean of
  -0.028, with 95% of shuffles inside [-0.82, +0.79]. None of the 500 reached the real
  +1.31.
- **No single storm carries it.** Dropping each of the 14 wet days in turn leaves the
  estimate between +1.15 and +1.76.
- **Both networks.** The effect is present in summer alone, +1.82, and in autumn
  alone, +1.17.

## 2. The morning, day by day

One row per working day, the 07-08 mean, with a wet morning defined as 0.5 mm or more
over the two hours. Thirty days, six of them wet:

| | seconds per hop | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| summer morning, dry, Monday to Thursday | +2.32 | [+1.33, +3.36] |
| autumn network | **+6.78** | **[+5.49, +8.30]** |
| Friday | -4.84 | [-7.52, -2.32] |
| wet morning | **+2.96** | **[+0.90, +4.69]** |

*Added 1 October: with Vilnius airport's reports deciding which mornings were wet, this
is +1.44 [-0.45, +3.41]. The hour-level estimate in section 1 is the robust one.*

**The autumn jump is not weather.** Holding rain fixed, the autumn morning still
costs 6.8 s more per hop than the summer one. Nor was autumn much wetter: 12.5% of
daytime hours were wet in the summer fortnight, 13.6% in early September and 18.8%
in late September.

**The Friday result survives** with rain held fixed: -4.84 s per hop, an interval
well clear of zero.

**The worst mornings were the wet ones.** Of the four worst autumn Monday-to-Thursday
mornings, three had rain: 17 September +14.13 (6.6 mm), 10 September +13.16 (0.8 mm)
and 23 September +13.12 (0.8 mm). The worst summer morning, 18 August at +4.37, had
3.2 mm. Of the four autumn Fridays, the two wet ones are the two highest.

**11 September is not weather.** The rerun hoped rain would explain it. It was dry all
day. It remains an unexplained low Friday.

## 3. What the rain was hiding

The [morning-settled](morning-settled-2026-09-30.md) check found a trend of -0.62 s
per week, 95% CI [-1.69, +0.49], and called the easing suggestive. Late September was
the wettest stretch of the record, and wet mornings sit well above dry ones, so the
rain was pulling the late weeks up.

Dry Monday-to-Thursday mornings only, autumn:

| | 2-9 Sep | 14-16 Sep | 21-29 Sep |
|---|---|---|---|
| mornings | 11.52, 10.10, 10.16, 12.23, 11.14 | 7.54, 9.93, 7.28 | 6.59, 9.59, 7.91, 6.86, 9.28 |
| mean | **+11.03** | +8.25 | **+8.05** |

Trend, weekday held fixed: **-1.06 s per hop per week, 95% CI [-1.86, -0.53]**,
thirteen mornings.

So there is an easing, and the interval now excludes zero. The table says more than
the trend line does: it is **mostly a step between the second and third school weeks**,
from about 11 s to about 8 s, and flat since. A 27% fall, and still about five times
the dry summer Monday-to-Thursday morning, +1.58.

The easing happened while sunrise moved from 06:25 to 07:15, so by late September the
first part of the 07-08 window is in darkness, and while mornings got 5°C colder
(13.8°C to 8.8°C at 07-08). Neither would make buses faster, so neither explains it. The simplest reading
is that people and drivers found their autumn routine in the first two weeks of term.
That is an interpretation, not a measurement.

## What this changes

- **Established: rain slows the network by about a third, in every window, and the
  effect lingers into the next hour.** Any comparison between two days, or two weeks,
  now has to ask whether one was wetter.
- **Established: the autumn morning jump is not weather.** +6.8 s per hop with rain
  held fixed.
- **Revised: the morning has eased, not merely settled.** On dry mornings it fell
  from about 11 to about 8 s per hop between the second and third week of term, and has
  held there. The 30 September document's "flat, within a band that allows a modest
  decline" was right about the direction and too cautious about the size, because the
  wet weeks were hiding it.
- **Unchanged:** the morning is still the worse peak, and still far above summer.

## Method, for reproduction

Everything above comes from [`code/weather_effect.py`](../code/weather_effect.py),
run from the repository root. It reads only published files, `analysis/trav-*.csv.gz`
and `weather/weather-*.csv`, and prints every number in this document. Bootstrap
seeds are fixed. Runtime about two minutes.

The within-day model is least squares of the hourly mean `lost_s` on the wet
indicator, with one level per day and one per hour-of-day in each period; intervals
resample whole days. The day-level model is least squares of the traversal-weighted
07-08 mean on an intercept, the autumn period, Friday and a wet morning; intervals
resample days, 20,000 times.
